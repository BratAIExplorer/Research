0 · The big idea: three rulebooks

MALAYSIA

Where you live

Sources say individuals are generally not taxed on gains from selling listed shares or ETFs, and normal Malaysian company dividends are not taxed again. A new 2% tax applies to dividends above RM100,000 a year (Budget 2025). Trading like a business can be taxed.

USA

The sneaky one

Malaysia has no tax treaty with the US. So US-listed funds lose 30% of their dividends to the US, and the W-8BEN form does not lower it for Malaysians (per the sources I read). Plus a "death tax" risk above about US$60,000. Section 5 explains.

EPF (KWSP)

Your first investment

Your EPF is already a huge fund you own. It paid 6.15% for 2025 and 6.3% for 2024, with a 2.5% yearly floor on the conventional account only. Know it before you add anything else.

Biggest question to answer first: will this money be spent in ringgit? Buying US-dollar funds adds a hidden risk: the exchange rate. EPF itself said a stronger ringgit against the US dollar lowered its 2025 equity returns. The same thing can shrink your US-fund gains.
Halal angle: many Malaysians want Shariah-compliant choices. EPF has a Shariah account, and Bursa has Shariah ETFs (the gold ETF is one). Which one is right is your call, not mine. The table below marks what I could and couldn't check.

TEAM DIP

Tap ↻

  • Cheaper prices feel smart. Some value investors do this on purpose.
  • Weak spot: you must spot the drop, buy while the news is scary, and not wait forever. Markets can rise for years without a 10% dip.
TEAM MIX

Regular + a bit extra in big falls

Tap ↻

  • Keep the automatic plan. Keep a small pot for big falls.
  • Rule: write it down before the fall.
  • Common and practical. Not proven to win.

The proof (go read it)

  • Vanguard compared "invest all now" with "spread it out" in US, UK and Australian markets. Investing all now won about two-thirds of the time over a year. Spreading out still beat sitting in cash most of the time. Sources give 66–68%. Search "Vanguard cost averaging invest now or temporarily hold your cash". I checked that the finding is real. I did not confirm the exact % on Vanguard's own page.
  • Charles Schwab compared pretend investors. One had perfect timing every year, one just invested on day one. Perfect timing won, but only a little. Read it ↗
  • Other side: Robert Shiller's work says high starting prices tend to mean weaker returns over the next 10–20 years. Price matters. It just doesn't tell you the month.

Same companies inside. Different "box". The box changes how much US tax you lose.

Fair challenge: the articles saying "buy Irish funds!" are often written by companies that sell them. The facts check out, but see how small the dollars are: RM10,000 in VOO at about 1% yield = RM100 of dividends. 30% vs 15% is RM30 vs RM15. The death-tax risk above US$60k is the bigger deal. ETF vs mutual fund (unit trust): the difference

In Malaysia a mutual fund is usually called a unit trust. Both are baskets. The difference is how you buy them and what they cost.

ETF (US, Irish or Bursa)Unit trust (mutual fund)
How you buyOn an exchange through a broker, like a shareFrom a bank, agent, platform or the manager directly

Who picks the holdingsUsually copies an index. Nobody is trying to winOften a manager picking. Some unit trusts are index funds too

5b · Keep it small: 3 to 5 funds, then stop

The list above is a menu. Nobody should order everything.

Why 3 to 5 max

  • Funds overlap. A world fund already owns the same big companies as an S&P 500 fund.
  • More funds = more fees to check, more tax paperwork, more things to watch, more urge to tinker.
  • Many people do fine with just 1 or 2.

Honest note: "3 to 5" is a common rule of thumb from index-investing teachers. I did not find a study proving it is the magic number.

A simple shape (an example, not advice)

  • Core: 1 or 2 broad funds (whole world, or US plus rest of world).
  • Safety: 1 bond or cash-like piece, if you need the money within a few years.
  • Spice: 0 to 2 small bets (gold, one country, one sector). A common rule of thumb keeps all spice to a small slice, like 5 to 10%.

Red flags in a shortlist

  • Two funds doing the same job (like VOO and CSPX).
  • Five funds all chasing the same hot theme.
  • A fund you can't explain in one sentence.
  • Buying it because it went up 100% last year.

Tip: tick funds in the table above. It counts them and warns about overlap.

How often should you review? Once a year.

Pick one fixed date. Twice a year at most. Not daily. Checking prices all the time makes people panic and sell at the wrong moment. I did not find a study proving the best rhythm. Once a year is the common default among index investors.

The 4-question yearly check

  1. Is my reason and timeline the same?
  2. Is any piece far off my plan (say more than 5 points)? If so, put new money into the smaller piece first. That is gentle "rebalancing".
  3. Are the fees still fine?
  4. Did a rule change (tax lines, limits)?

Pick your date: Shortly after EPF announces its yearly dividend (late February or March), or before you file your Malaysian tax return.

Check sooner only if…

  • Your job or income changes, or you move country.
  • A goal is about 5 years away. Then slowly shift toward safer pieces.
  • A fund closes or its fees jump.
  • You need the cash.

Don't

  • Sell because of a headline.
  • Chase last year's winner.
  • Add a 6th fund "just because".

Three quick tools. They point the way. They are not a tax return.

Your Malaysian basics

The 2% dividend rule

US "death tax" gauge

How to read it

  • The line is about US$60,000 of US assets (this includes US-listed ETFs) when a non-US person dies. Above it, the US can tax the excess at up to 40%.
  • Irish-listed funds are generally not counted as US assets, which is why people look at them.
  • I did not confirm whether any treaty changes this for you. Assume it applies and ask a tax specialist near the line.

Cheat-sheet

  • Selling Bursa shares and ETFs: sources say individual investors are generally not taxed on the gain. Trading often like a business can change that.
  • Malaysian dividends: normal single-tier dividends are not taxed again for you. Since Budget 2025 there is a 2% tax on dividend income above RM100,000 a year.
  • Overseas income: foreign-sourced income received by resident individuals is exempt until 31 Dec 2036, with conditions. But the US still takes its 30% first on US-listed funds.
  • PRS: contributions can earn up to RM3,000 tax relief, extended to assessment year 2030.
  • Moving money abroad: Bank Negara's foreign exchange rules set what residents may do. See the BNM link in section 9.

Things I could not confirm

  • Whether gains on listed shares could become taxable. One source said a capital gains tax on Bursa-listed shares was discussed but seen as unlikely in Budget 2026. Watch for news, and check LHDN.
  • One expat guide says Budget 2026 widened the foreign-income exemption to capital gains on foreign assets. I found no LHDN source. Don't rely on it.
  • The "taxed at source" condition for the foreign-income exemption comes from one commercial guide. Read LHDN's guidelines on income received from abroad (section 9).
  • Exactly which dividends count towards the RM100,000 line (for example unit trust payouts or foreign dividends). I did not confirm.
  • Current rules of EPF's Members Investment Scheme. Sources disagree (a 30% cap and RM5,000 over Basic Savings appear most often). Check i-Akaun.
  • The BNM limit if you have ringgit borrowing. The RM1 million a year figure is from an older bank FAQ. The latest BNM notices are dated Oct 2025.
  • Who is allowed to buy which ASNB funds. Check ASNB.
  • Bursa ETF fees, trading volume and which are Shariah-compliant today. Check Bursa and each fund's fact sheet.

9 · Real places to check everything

Official or independent first. Broker articles are labelled, because they sell things.

Before giving anyone money, ask 4 things

  1. Is this firm licensed by the Securities Commission Malaysia or Bank Negara? (Check their registers and alert lists.)
  2. What are all the fees, in dollars a year on my amount?
  3. What is the worst year shown in the product document?
  4. Do you get paid if I say yes?
Red flags: "guaranteed" returns, 15–20% "target" returns, WhatsApp or Telegram "tip groups", a countdown timer, "only a few slots", pressure to decide today. Scammers often pretend to be licensed firms, so find the firm's number yourself and call it. A friend's private "opportunity" is a stranger's until you check the licence.

Also be careful with: savings-plus-insurance bundles. They can have long lock-ins and costs. Ask for the full cost table and what you'd get back if you stopped early.
